I nearly didn't go. The Cinque Terre has become shorthand for overtourism — day-trippers funnelled through Vernazza's single street like sand through an hourglass, selfie queues at the harbour. Every seasoned traveler I know had the same advice: skip it.

They were wrong, and so was I. The mistake isn't the place; it's the timing. I arrived on the 14th of October and found five of the most beautiful villages in the Mediterranean operating at perhaps a fifth of summer capacity, with the sea still warm enough that the old men of Monterosso were swimming their morning lengths.

## The out-of-phase principle

This trip crystallised a rule I now apply everywhere: **the more famous the place, the more the calendar matters — and older solo travelers hold the trump card, because we can go whenever we like.** No school holidays, no leave approvals. The shoulder season isn't a compromise for us; it's the entire strategy.

October in the Cinque Terre means fourteen-hour village days without the heat (it was 21°C most afternoons), room rates at half of July's, and restaurant tables that don't need booking — which matters when you're one person and reservations engines think in pairs.

## Boats before trains, trains before trails

The received wisdom is to hike between the villages. The coastal path is glorious — and parts of it are steep, exposed, and stepped in the hundreds. Be honest with yourself, then do what I did:

- **The ferry is the best sightseeing in Liguria.** The villages were built to be seen from the sea; the boat between Monterosso and Riomaggiore shows you all five stacked on their cliffs for a few euros. Runs until early November, sea permitting.
- **The train** connects all five villages in minutes and removes all pressure. Miss one? Another comes in twenty minutes.
- **Hike selectively.** I walked the high sanctuary trail from Volastra down through the vineyards to Manarola — gentler gradients, terraced views the coastal path can't match, and I met eleven people in three hours. The famous lower path from Monterosso to Vernazza I did in the cool of 8 a.m., took it slowly with poles, and had it nearly to myself.

## A village to sleep in

Day-trippers see the Cinque Terre between 10 and 4. Sleep in the villages and you get the other eighteen hours. I based in **Manarola** — quieter than Vernazza, prettier than Riomaggiore (I'll take the letters) — in a room up sixty-two steps that the host's brother carried my bag up without being asked.

At 7 p.m., when the last day crowds drained toward La Spezia, the village exhaled. Fishermen flipped their boats in the tiny square. The enoteca on the main street poured local Sciacchetrà dessert wine and put out free focaccia because, the owner said, October guests are guests, August guests are cargo.

> Famous places haven't been ruined. They've been mis-scheduled. Arrive out of phase and the postcard is still in there, intact, waiting.

## Practical notes

Buy the Cinque Terre Treno card only on days you'll ride three or more times or hike the toll trails; otherwise single tickets win in the quiet season. Every village involves some climbing — Corniglia is 382 steps from its station (there's a shuttle bus; take it and feel no shame). Trekking poles transformed the trails for me and no one under forty knows what they're missing.

And carry your swimsuit through October. The sea holds its summer warmth long after the crowds have lost their nerve.
