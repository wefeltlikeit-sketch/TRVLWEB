For centuries, Sarlat's Place du Marché aux Oies was exactly what the name says: the square where geese were sold, in a town whose entire economy once ran on foie gras, walnuts, and truffles. The live geese are gone. In their place stand three bronze ones by Lalanne — polished gold on the heads and backs where forty years of hands have rested.

Come on a Wednesday or Saturday and the square fills with market stalls; come any other morning before nine and you get the geese, the limestone, and nobody at all.

## The advice

Every French market town has a square like this — named for what used to be sold there, quietly explaining the whole town if you stop to read it. Look up the square names before you visit: *aux Oies* (geese), *aux Noix* (walnuts), *de la Boucherie* (you can guess).

The "still searching" part: I have yet to find the walnut oil vendor an old man in this square described to me in detail — "third stall past the church, but only in October." I'll be back in October.
