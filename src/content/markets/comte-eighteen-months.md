Every French market has its cheese van, and the good ones have a queue of locals who know exactly what they want and will not be rushed. The Sarlat Wednesday market has three. The one to find is at the bottom of the Rue de la République, where the wheels of Comté are stacked like spare tyres.

The vendor cuts a sliver from the eighteen-month wheel and hands it over on the knife — crystalline, nutty, faintly of hazelnuts and hay. There is a ritual to this: you taste, you consider, you look at the middle distance for a moment, and then you ask for more than you planned to buy.

## The lesson

Cheese, unlike sausage, travels — hard cheeses are generally allowed into the U.S., and a vacuum-sealed wedge survives a checked bag with no complaints. This particular wedge never got the chance to prove it. It was opened "just to try with lunch" in a layby above the Dordogne river, and that was the end of it.

Should have bought two. Next time I will, and the second one gets vacuum-packed on the spot — most vendors will do it if you ask.
