The Saturday market in Apt takes over the whole old town, and somewhere between the third olive stall and a man selling nothing but espadrilles, there was a table of saucissons in every flavour Provence could think of. Donkey. Fig. Roquefort. And — because this is the Luberon — lavender.

I bought one. Of course I bought one. It rode in the top of the bag for eleven days, wrapped like contraband, which — as U.S. Customs and Border Protection would later explain to me, patiently, at a small steel table — is exactly what it was.

## What I learned

Cured meats from the EU do not enter the United States, no matter how honestly you declare them or how small and lavender-scented they are. Declare them anyway: the fine for the undeclared version is real money, while the declared version costs you only the sausage and a little dignity.

The correct move, I now know, is to eat the sausage in France, on a bridge, with bread from the same market. Consider this entry a memorial, and a public service announcement.
